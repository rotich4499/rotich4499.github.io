Research

My research uses density functional theory to connect atomic structure with the properties and activity of functional materials.

Electronic structure and band gaps

Silicon in the diamond structure and benzene

I study electronic states through band structures, total density of states (DOS) and projected density of states (PDOS), using the projections to identify the contributions of particular atoms and orbitals. For benzene, my interests also include molecular energy levels and the distinction between the HOMO–LUMO gap and gaps obtained from total-energy differences.

Why can an insulating perovskite appear metallic in a DFT calculation?

Ongoing investigation

I investigate the origin of band gaps in perovskite oxides: why a calculation constrained to an oversimplified structural or magnetic description can predict a metal, and how allowing appropriate symmetry breaking can reveal an insulating state. My focus is on the roles of atomic structure, spin arrangements and orbital character, examined through band structures and DOS/PDOS.

Background reading: Varignon, Bibes and Zunger, Origin of band gaps in 3d perovskite oxides, Nature Communications 10, 1658 (2019).

MSc research project

First-Principles Investigation of Electronic, Optical, Redox Properties of Spinel Oxides CoMn₂O₄ and CuCo₂O₄ for Photocatalytic Water Splitting

Research in progress · The Technical University of Kenya

This comparative study examines how bulk and surface properties influence the potential of two spinel oxides for photocatalytic water splitting. The work combines structural and electronic calculations with optical response and oxygen-evolution reaction energetics.

Questions I investigate

  • How do structure and magnetic ordering affect the stability and electronic properties of the oxides?
  • How do optical response and band-edge alignment relate to water-splitting applications?
  • Which surface terminations and adsorption pathways influence oxygen-evolution activity?

Computational approach

I use VASP and Quantum ESPRESSO to study bulk stability, electronic structure, optical response, surface stability, and adsorption of O*, OH*, OOH* and H₂O. Computational hydrogen electrode concepts connect adsorption energetics to reaction free-energy pathways and catalytic overpotential.

Bachelor’s research project

Effects of Temperature and Intrinsic Structural Defects on Mechanical Properties of Niobium Nitride

Bachelor of Technology final-year project · The Technical University of Kenya · Report dated September 2021

I used Quantum ESPRESSO calculations and molecular dynamics to investigate the bulk modulus of rock-salt NbN, comparing a pristine crystal with a nitrogen-vacancy supercell and structures relaxed at different temperatures. The workflow included convergence tests, structural relaxation and Birch–Murnaghan equation-of-state fitting.
