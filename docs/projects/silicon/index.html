Silicon: band gaps and fractional charge

PBEsol · DZP · Silicon in the diamond structure

This study compares a band-structure gap with gaps calculated from total-energy changes when electrons are added or removed. Silicon also provides a way to examine how the result changes with the charge step and the size of the simulation cell.

Results and original figures from my silicon and diamond presentation, 14 October 2026. These are reported calculation results; the calculations have not been rerun for this website.

Band structure

The reported Kohn–Sham gap is 0.45602719 eV and indirect. An indirect gap has its valence-band maximum and conduction-band minimum at different crystal momenta.

What the three gaps measure

Kohn–Sham gap
The difference between the conduction-band minimum and valence-band maximum of the neutral cell.

EgKS = ECBM − EVBM

Finite-step derivative estimate
The difference between average energy slopes on either side of the neutral electron count. Reducing δ probes a smaller charge interval.

G(δ) = [E(N + δ) − 2E(N) + E(N − δ)] / δ

Integer ΔSCF gap
The total-energy difference for adding and removing a whole electron. It is the δ = 1 case of the finite-step expression.

G(1) = E(N − 1) − 2E(N) + E(N + 1)

Here N = 8 is the neutral valence-electron count in the reference primitive cell, and δ is a charge step in electrons. The derivative gap in the zero-step limit and an estimate at a finite δ are distinct quantities.

Fractional-charge curvature

Compare the calculated energy with a straight line joining adjacent integer-electron states. The silicon energies lie below that line; the presentation associates this convex behaviour with a tendency to delocalise fractional charge.

Why cell size matters

The integer ΔSCF result falls as the simulation cell grows. A value obtained by adding one electron to a two-atom primitive cell therefore needs a cell-size check before it is interpreted as a bulk result.

Reducing the charge step

In the primitive cell, the finite-step estimate changes from 2.438585 eV at δ = 1 to 0.458000 eV at δ = 0.001. The latter is about 2.0 meV above the reported KS gap.

Full charge-step scan and 2 × 2 × 2 supercell check

Following the analysis

  1. Build the diamond-structure silicon cell and record the numerical settings.
  2. Run the neutral calculation and identify the band edges from the band structure.
  3. Collect total energies at N and at N ± δ, using a consistent geometry and recording the charged-cell treatment.
  4. Compare the finite-step estimates as δ is reduced.
  5. Repeat the size comparison with larger cells, comparing each derivative estimate with the corresponding cell’s KS gap.

These steps explain the analysis shown in the slides. To begin with supplied SIESTA inputs and reference outputs, use the separate silicon SCF, convergence and relaxation course.

Charged periodic-cell energies depend on the simulation setup and size. The primitive-cell integer result is a finite-cell calculation, and the values here are not experimental gaps.
