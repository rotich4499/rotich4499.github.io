Band structure

The reported PBEsol Kohn–Sham gap is 4.0854 eV and indirect. This is the neutral-cell band-edge difference used as the reference in the charge-step comparison.

What the three gaps measure

Kohn–Sham gap
The difference between the conduction-band minimum and valence-band maximum of the neutral cell.

EgKS = ECBM − EVBM

Finite-step derivative estimate
The difference between average energy slopes on either side of the neutral electron count. Reducing δ probes a smaller charge interval.

G(δ) = [E(N + δ) − 2E(N) + E(N − δ)] / δ

Integer ΔSCF gap
The total-energy difference for adding and removing a whole electron. It is the δ = 1 case of the finite-step expression.

G(1) = E(N − 1) − 2E(N) + E(N + 1)

Here N = 8 is the neutral valence-electron count in the reference primitive cell, and δ is a charge step in electrons. The derivative gap in the zero-step limit and an estimate at a finite δ are distinct quantities.

Fractional charge and curvature

The calculated fractional-charge energies lie below the straight interpolation between adjacent integer-electron states. The presentation interprets this convex curvature in terms of a delocalisation tendency.

Following the analysis

  1. Set up the two-atom carbon diamond cell using the reported lattice and numerical settings.
  2. Obtain the neutral band structure and identify its valence and conduction edges.
  3. Compare total energies at N = 8 and at N ± δ, keeping the geometry consistent and recording the treatment of a charged periodic cell.
  4. Calculate G(δ), reduce the charge step and compare the estimates with the neutral-cell KS gap.

These steps describe the slide analysis. The slides do not include a complete diamond input/output bundle. Charged-cell and size effects need to be assessed before interpreting an integer ΔSCF value as a bulk gap; the reported numbers are not experimental measurements.
