Benzene: molecular gaps and fractional charge

Gas-phase C₆H₆ · SIESTA · PBE via LibXC · DZP

This study compares the gap between molecular orbitals with gaps obtained from changes in total energy. Fractional-electron calculations show how the estimate depends on the charge interval used to probe the energy curve.

What the three gaps measure

Kohn–Sham gap
The difference between the neutral molecule’s lowest unoccupied and highest occupied orbital energies.

EgKS = εLUMO − εHOMO

Finite-step derivative estimate
The difference between average energy slopes on either side of the neutral electron count. Reducing δ probes a smaller charge interval.

G(δ) = [E(N + δ) − 2E(N) + E(N − δ)] / δ

Integer ΔSCF gap
The total-energy difference for adding and removing a whole electron. It is the δ = 1 case of the finite-step expression.

G(1) = E(N − 1) − 2E(N) + E(N + 1)

Here N = 30 is the neutral valence-electron count in the reference molecule, and δ is a charge step in electrons. The derivative gap in the zero-step limit and an estimate at a finite δ are distinct quantities.

Following the analysis

  1. Prepare the neutral molecule, choose the numerical settings and record the geometry.
  2. Obtain the neutral HOMO and LUMO energies.
  3. Calculate the 29-, 30- and 31-electron energies at the same geometry for the integer ΔSCF comparison.
  4. Calculate energies at 30 ± δ for progressively smaller charge steps.
  5. Evaluate G(δ) and compare it with the neutral KS gap, checking that total energies are sufficiently precise for the small differences.

These steps explain the presentation’s analysis. A complete benzene input/output bundle has not yet been added. The orbital gap, total-energy gap and optical excitation gap describe different quantities; the slides do not provide an experimental benchmark.
