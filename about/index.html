About

I am Charles Kipchumba Rotich, a Kenyan computational physicist working in materials science. I am pursuing an MSc in Physics at The Technical University of Kenya, where I also serve as a Graduate Research Assistant.

My current research examines CoMn₂O₄ and CuCo₂O₄ spinel oxides for photocatalytic water splitting. I am undertaking a five-month Erasmus+ KA171 research exchange at the University of Cantabria in Santander, Spain.

Background

I graduated from The Technical University of Kenya in 2022 with a Bachelor of Technology in Technical and Applied Physics, earning First Class Honours. My specialisation included materials science and computational physics.

My bachelor’s final-year project studied the effects of temperature and intrinsic structural defects on the mechanical properties of niobium nitride, using first-principles calculations and molecular dynamics.

My experience includes computational materials modelling with the TUK Material Modelling Team, research-cloud workflows at KENET, and destructive and non-destructive materials testing at the Materials Testing and Research Division. I have also guided students through electronic-structure simulation workflows.

Scientific training

I have participated in APhRICA advanced physics training at the University of Rwanda / ICTP-EAIFR over two years, alongside the ICTP Physics Without Frontiers workshop in Nairobi, the CUEA DFT School and Data Science Africa in Nyeri.

Tools I use

VASP, Quantum ESPRESSO, SIESTA, ABINIT, Python and Linux-based high-performance computing environments. My work also involves MPI, Slurm and scientific data analysis.

Sharing the workflow

I am developing step-by-step learning resources so students and researchers can understand the methods, inspect the files and reproduce the calculations for themselves.
